<p><strong>Choose FreshBooks</strong> if the business has regular client billing, can justify a paid system, and needs a public path beyond a free-product ceiling. On the primary U.S. <a href="https://www.freshbooks.com/pricing">FreshBooks pricing page</a> checked July 27, Lite is $23 per month for 5 billable clients, Plus is $43 for 50, and Premium is $70 for unlimited clients. That makes Plus the honest starting comparison for a contractor already billing six or more customers.</p>

<p><strong>Choose Zoho Invoice</strong> if the operation is genuinely small and $0 matters more than extra headroom. Zoho's <a href="https://www.zoho.com/us/invoice/pricing/">official pricing page</a> says the product is free, but it also states the limits: up to 2 users, a maximum of three projects, and up to 500 invoices per year. That isn't unlimited invoicing with a few footnotes. It's a usable small-business tool with a boundary that has to be planned.</p>

<p><strong>The deciding nuance:</strong> FreshBooks is a paid billing and small-business accounting choice. Zoho Invoice is a free invoicing choice. Neither verdict is a universal winner. CSH judgment: if the real buying problem is job-level labor, materials, subcontractors, change orders, retainage, schedule, or dispatch, don't let this comparison make that decision for you. Run those exact scenarios in the product category built for them.</p>

## Price is the first workflow test

<p>FreshBooks has a public monthly plan ladder on the main buying path. The checked page lists Lite at $23 per month for 5 billable clients, Plus at $43 for 50, Premium at $70 for unlimited clients, and Select as a consultation. It also lists Team Members at $11 per user per month as an add-on on the public plans. Those are the stable planning numbers used here. A contractor with seven active billable customers should not base the decision on Lite just because $23 looks attractive. The price that applies is Plus at $43, before any added users or other optional services.</p>

<p>The same page advertises a limited 90% promotion. FreshBooks' <a href="https://www.freshbooks.com/policies/buy-now-offer">offer terms</a> checked July 27 say it runs for six months and ends at 11:59 PM EST that day for eligible first-time purchasers. That's a checkout event, not the price used in this comparison. Its official <a href="https://www.freshbooks.com/pricing-sm">pricing-sm page</a> displayed a different rate card and promotion during the same check. Don't blend the two pages, annual discounts, and a short offer into a pretend permanent monthly cost. Confirm the buying path and checkout price for your account before purchase.</p>

<p>Zoho Invoice's price posture is simpler and stricter. The vendor says $0, then names the limits directly: 2 users, three projects, and 500 invoices a year. It also says invoices carry Powered by Zoho Invoice branding. There isn't a paid capacity upgrade inside the Zoho Invoice pricing page to solve a contractor's fourth project or third user. Zoho directs a growing business to find a more powerful fit. That could be a broader Zoho product or a different platform, but it should be an intentional comparison before the limit interferes with billing.</p>

<p>The invoice limit needs a real calculation. Divide 500 by 52 and the result is roughly 9.6 invoices a week. That isn't a forecast. It's a quick way to see why volume matters. A contractor who invoices one larger project at milestones may have lots of room. A maintenance business that invoices every visit, bills deposits, and follows work with separate materials or balance invoices can consume the allowance much faster. Pull the last 90 days of sent invoices, multiply by four, add a busy-season buffer, and compare that number with 500. Do the same with active projects and named users, rather than assuming a helper or technician won't need access.</p>

<p>The FreshBooks break-even question is different: who owns the $23 or $43 recurring charge, and what is it replacing? If an office manager uses time, expenses, estimates, online payments, recurring invoices, and reminders every week, a paid billing workflow can be easy to defend. If the owner sends four invoices a month and doesn't use the rest of the work, the subscription may be paying for unused capacity. CSH judgment: don't pay for a bigger plan because it feels more professional. Pay when the tested workflow prevents enough re-entry, missed billing, or payment follow-up to earn its seat in the budget.</p>

## Both can move a completed job toward payment, but their paths differ

<p>FreshBooks' <a href="https://www.freshbooks.com/features">features page</a> describes professional invoices that can include tracked time and expenses, along with billing, online payments, reminders, expenses, payroll, reporting, proposals, estimates, and team management. Its pricing table also identifies recurring invoices, late-payment reminders, deposits, time tracking, expenses, accountant access, and reports across its plans. That points to a connected small-business billing workflow: record billable activity, turn it into an estimate or invoice, accept payment, and see the result in reports. Read the <a href="/freshbooks-review/">FreshBooks review</a> beside this comparison if the accounting side of that workflow is carrying more of the decision.</p>

<p>A service contractor can test that claim with a normal repair job. Create the customer. Add a quoted labor line and a material expense. Send an estimate. Convert it or recreate it as an invoice according to the actual office process. Add the payment method customers use. Trigger a reminder. Then hand the resulting record to the bookkeeper or accountant who will use the reports. The test isn't complete when the invoice PDF looks good. It is complete when payment, expense, time, client record, and reporting handoff match the current office process without a spreadsheet cleanup.</p>

<p>Zoho Invoice's <a href="https://www.zoho.com/us/invoice/">product page</a> documents a similar invoice-layer loop: quotes that can become invoices, recurring invoices, payment reminders, billable expenses, time tracking, a customer portal, and business reports. Its official contractor page adds estimates, project expenses included on client invoices, online payments, and time tracking. For a small contractor, that is a real amount of capability at $0. It is more than a static invoice generator. The <a href="/zoho-invoice-review/">Zoho Invoice review</a> covers the product on its own for a buyer who is not choosing between these two paths.</p>

<p>The Zoho test should be just as strict. Create a genuine estimate with scope and payment terms. Add an expense that needs to be billed back. Record billable time if the business sells labor that way. Send the invoice through the actual payment path. Open the customer portal as if you were the customer. Confirm the reminder and invoice delivery settings. Then look at the user count, project count, and annual invoice pace after the test data is in place. If the product fits only by treating all active work as one project or by denying an office helper access, it doesn't fit.</p>

<p>There is an important difference in buying posture here. FreshBooks asks you to choose capacity upfront through its plans. Zoho Invoice lets you start without a bill, then asks you to stay within fixed limits. Neither approach is automatically better. Paying early can be wasteful for a tiny business. Replacing an invoicing system in the middle of a busy season can also be wasteful. The better choice is the one that gives you room for the work already booked, not the work you hope stays small forever.</p>

## FreshBooks project profitability is useful information, not a construction job-costing promise

<p>FreshBooks lists project profitability on Premium and Select in its pricing comparison. Its <a href="https://www.freshbooks.com/hub/feature-news/easily-track-every-projects-performance">project profitability explanation</a> calls it a reporting tool that brings together project income, costs, and profits, including time and expenses tracked to projects by team members. That can help a service shop ask useful questions: which type of work has margin, where team time is going, and whether a job is tracking against an estimate.</p>

<p>Don't flatten that into the phrase "construction job costing." Those words carry a harder operating test. A contractor may need every labor burden, purchase, material receipt, subcontractor commitment, allowance, change order, retention amount, and progress billing event tied to the same job record. FreshBooks' project profitability description is evidence for project reporting, not evidence that every construction accounting control is covered. The distinction matters because an owner can see a margin number and still be unable to trust it for a bid, draw, or job-close decision. Contractors who need that deeper accounting question should also read the <a href="/small-contractor-bookkeeping-software/">small contractor bookkeeping software guide</a>.</p>

<p>Use a source-of-truth test before buying FreshBooks for profitability. Take one completed job with labor, material, a subcontractor, an approved scope change, and a deposit or progress payment. Ask the person who closes the books to identify where each cost and revenue event will enter. Ask the project lead to identify what report they need every Friday. If the team has to keep the true job cost in another system, FreshBooks can still be the invoice and small-business accounting layer. It just shouldn't be sold internally as the one financial record for the job.</p>

<p>Zoho Invoice has a narrower constraint before that discussion even starts: its pricing page caps projects at three. Its contractor material supports estimates, expenses, payment reminders, and time tracking, which are useful billing inputs. CSH judgment: a contractor whose margin control depends on job records across several active sites should treat the three-project cap as a stop sign for this particular product, not as a challenge to work around with loose naming conventions.</p>

## Practical contractor scenarios make the split clearer

<p><strong>Scenario one: the solo electrician with eight recurring customers.</strong> The business has service calls, a few maintenance agreements, and a bookkeeper who needs the records. The owner bills more than five clients, so FreshBooks Lite is already the wrong card. FreshBooks Plus is the fair comparison at $43 per month, plus any necessary team access. Zoho Invoice could still be free if the user and project limits fit, but the owner should calculate invoice volume from maintenance visits and customer count before choosing it. FreshBooks is the cleaner shortlist when the owner wants the paid billing route and expects regular customer activity to keep growing. For the broader financial decision, see the <a href="/best-accounting-software-for-electrical-contractors/">accounting software guide for electrical contractors</a>.</p>

<p><strong>Scenario two: the new landscaping operator with one owner and a few quoted jobs.</strong> The work involves estimates, deposits, final invoices, simple expenses, and a tight cash budget. Zoho Invoice is the better first shortlist if the business can operate under 2 users, three projects, and 500 invoices a year. The owner should still run a payment and customer-portal test before importing contacts. FreshBooks belongs on the list only if regular billing or reporting use justifies paying from the beginning. If proposal approval is the real choke point, compare the <a href="/best-proposal-software-for-contractors/">best proposal software for contractors</a> before adding billing software.</p>

<p><strong>Scenario three: the HVAC company adding a dispatcher and two technicians.</strong> Here, neither answer should be automatic. Three people who need product access exceed Zoho Invoice's 2-user limit. FreshBooks can cover the paid small-business billing layer, but each required team member affects the real cost because FreshBooks lists Team Members as an add-on. The more important question is whether dispatch, scheduling, job records, and billing need to live together. If they do, compare field-service systems before buying either invoice product and building a workaround around it. The <a href="/field-service-management-mobile-app/">field-service mobile app guide</a> and <a href="/jobber-review/">Jobber review</a> are better next reads for that team.</p>

<p><strong>Scenario four: the remodeler managing several jobs at once.</strong> Zoho Invoice's maximum of three projects is the direct issue. FreshBooks can report project profitability on listed higher plans, but that is not enough evidence to make a construction accounting decision. This buyer needs a live demo built around the job's costs, scope changes, customer billing schedule, and closeout requirements. The right outcome may be to skip both, even if either can send an attractive invoice.</p>

<p>These examples aren't rules about trade size. They are a way to separate an invoice need from an operating-system need. A high-volume solo contractor may need FreshBooks sooner than a larger company with a few long projects. A small company may fit Zoho Invoice for years if billing is sparse. Measure the work, then choose the tool.</p>

## Independent user evidence supports a trial, not blind trust

<p>The independent feedback checked for this comparison is useful but not contractor-specific. The <a href="https://www.capterra.com/p/142390/FreshBooks/reviews/">FreshBooks Capterra reviews page</a> included praise for straightforward invoicing, expense management, automation, time tracking, and online payments. It also included complaints about restricted reporting workflows, inconsistent expense categorization, payment fees, limited integrations for some buyers, and a mobile receipt-upload issue. That is a practical message for contractors: don't assume the report or connection you need behaves the way the product page implies. Test it with your own categories, bank process, receipts, and accountant handoff.</p>

<p>The <a href="https://www.capterra.com/p/163114/Zoho-Invoice/reviews/">Zoho Invoice Capterra reviews page</a> had a different pattern. Reviewers praised the free price, quick custom invoices, payment reminders, and invoice creation. The trade-offs were equally concrete: examples described a learning curve, crowded navigation, integration friction, payment setup trouble, currency restrictions after invoicing, and reconciliation or reporting pain in more detailed records. That doesn't make Zoho Invoice bad. It tells a contractor to test the exact payment gateway, export, report, and customer setup before committing open receivables to the system.</p>

<p>Neither review page is a contractor survey, and a review example isn't a promise about your shop. The pages contain feedback from varied industries, with incentives disclosed on some reviews. The useful conclusion is narrower: both products can make basic billing easier for the right buyer, and both have setup or reporting details that deserve a real trial. That is why the migration plan below matters more than a platform score.</p>

## Implementation and migration: make the exit plan before the import

<p>Start with a controlled slice of data, not every customer and every old invoice. Pick five recent jobs that include a straight invoice, an estimate, a partial payment, an expense to be billed through, a slow-paying customer, and recurring work if the business has it. Build the records in the candidate product while the old process remains the source of truth. This exposes missing fields, duplicate customer handling, invoice template problems, payment setup gaps, and reports the office can't use before the team has to fix them under pressure. This rollout needs an owner, a short training plan, a documented data migration checklist, and a cutover date. Without those four pieces, an otherwise good invoice app can stall in the office and leave the field working from old records.</p>

<p>For FreshBooks, make the plan choice part of implementation. Count billable clients before importing. Decide who needs a paid team seat and who only needs reports from the bookkeeper. Check the client portal, payment options, estimates, recurring invoices, reminders, expenses, and accountant access that appear on the selected plan. Then review the plan at the end of the trial using the active customer count, not the leads list. A Lite trial is not a successful rollout if the company will need Plus the week it goes live.</p>

<p>For Zoho Invoice, write the ceiling on the rollout checklist: 2 users, three projects, and 500 invoices a year. Name the person who watches each number. Set a review point well before the first limit, such as after the busy season or after a defined invoice count that leaves room for corrections. Export sample customer, invoice, and report data during the pilot so the office knows what a future move will require. A free system is a good place to start only if leaving it later is a planned decision rather than an emergency.</p>

<p>In either product, document who can issue a credit, edit an invoice, mark payment received, send a reminder, and make a new customer. Decide whether the field creates an estimate, whether the office turns it into the invoice, and how expenses reach the billing record. A tool doesn't repair unclear ownership. It just makes unclear ownership faster and easier to repeat. If technicians are the source of billable hours, use the <a href="/best-time-tracking-software-contractors/">time tracking software guide</a> to test that part of the workflow before a billing rollout.</p>
