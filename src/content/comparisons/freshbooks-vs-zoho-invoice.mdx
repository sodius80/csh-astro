<p><strong>Choose FreshBooks</strong> if the contractor business already has regular client billing, paid users, and enough invoice volume that Lite's client ceiling would interrupt operations. Its [official pricing page](https://www.freshbooks.com/pricing) lists Lite at $23/mo for 5 billable clients, Plus at $43/mo for 50, Premium at $70/mo for unlimited clients, and Select as a custom quote.</p>

<p><strong>Choose Zoho Invoice</strong> if the business is very small and the priority is professional invoicing at $0. Zoho's [official pricing page](https://www.zoho.com/us/invoice/pricing/) lists 2 users, 3 projects, and 500 invoices per year. Those are workable guardrails for a new or low-volume operator, but they are not a growth plan.</p>

<p><strong>The nuance:</strong> both tools handle the invoice layer. Neither one handles contractor job costing, AIA billing, retainage, change orders, scheduling, or dispatch. A contractor buying software to fix those jobs should skip this head-to-head and start with the <a href="/best-invoicing-software-contractors/">best invoicing software for contractors</a> list or a field-service system instead.</p>

## The real split is paid workflow versus a hard $0-tier ceiling

<p>FreshBooks asks a contractor to pay for invoicing from the start. In exchange, it publishes a tier ladder that begins with Lite, moves to Plus as client count rises, and reaches Premium for unlimited billable clients. That is easier to budget than a sales-led quote, but the headline price is only useful if it matches the business. A one-person service shop with six active billable clients is not a Lite buyer. It is already looking at Plus.</p>

<p>Zoho Invoice has the opposite posture. The software costs $0, and its official page lists invoices, estimates, recurring invoices, payment reminders, expenses, time tracking, projects, and a customer portal. The trade is capacity. A contractor who brings in an office helper, runs more than three active projects, or sends invoices at a pace that reaches 500 in a year has to make another software decision. That is fine when the business is still proving its process. It gets expensive in time if the team waits until the limit blocks a normal workday.</p>

<p>FreshBooks has its own ceiling. It remains invoicing and small-business accounting software, not construction accounting. Its [FreshBooks review](/freshbooks-review/) explains the missing job-cost, inventory, progress-billing, and AIA workflow. Zoho Invoice has the same category boundary, with the added operating limits covered in the <a href="/zoho-invoice-review/">Zoho Invoice review</a>.</p>

<p>The FreshBooks math is plain. A contractor with four active billing clients can start at $23/mo. A contractor with eight should compare from $43/mo, because Plus is the first listed plan that covers 50 billable clients. Add $11/mo for every team member who needs access. The buyer should price the plan that the business will use in month six, not the lowest card on the page.</p>

<p>The break-even test for FreshBooks is practical: the monthly cost is $23/mo only while Lite fits, so ask if faster invoicing or one avoided payment chase each month can recover that cost. If the answer is yes, compare FreshBooks Plus from $43/mo before the sixth billable client forces the choice.</p>

<p>For Zoho Invoice, the $0 price holds only while its published limits leave room. If the paid break-even doesn't work, use Zoho Invoice while its limits still fit the business.</p>

<p>Zoho Invoice's $0 price is real, and it is the right price for a contractor whose work fits the published limits. The problem starts when $0 is treated as an unlimited price. At 500 invoices a year, the business averages just under 10 invoices a week. A one-truck operator with a few larger jobs can stay under that line for a long time. A service business that invoices every visit, collects deposits, or sends progress invoices can arrive there much sooner. Count invoices from the last 90 days, multiply by four, and leave room for busy season before treating the free plan as the long-term answer.</p>

## What each product changes on a contractor's day

<p>FreshBooks fits a contractor whose billing starts with time, a client, or a project and needs to end in a sent invoice without rebuilding the information. The vendor's pricing page places expense tracking, estimates, online payment options, and client records inside the plan structure. Its listed tiers also show the client-count decision before an owner has invested time in a demo. That matters for service contractors who need a clean line from work completed to invoice sent.</p>

<p>Zoho Invoice puts more capability into the $0 tier than a bare invoice generator. Its [product page](https://www.zoho.com/us/invoice/) lists online payments and automated reminders, while the pricing page lists time tracking, projects, expenses, recurring invoices, and a customer portal. For a new electrical, HVAC, plumbing, or landscaping business, that can cover the core office loop: quote the work, send the bill, record the payment, and keep the customer record. The problem is not that the product lacks an invoice button. The problem is that it has no room to absorb the next stage of the business without a separate decision.</p>

<p>Neither product should be chosen to answer, "Did this job make money after labor, material, subcontractor cost, and change orders?" That is a job-costing question. A contractor who needs that answer should not try to force it into a lightweight invoice workflow. The <a href="/small-contractor-bookkeeping-software/">small contractor bookkeeping software guide</a> is a better starting point for that buyer.</p>

## User evidence points to different kinds of risk

<p>Zoho Invoice has stronger usable review detail in this check. Its [Capterra review page](https://www.capterra.com/p/163114/Zoho-Invoice/reviews/) showed recurring praise for getting invoices out quickly, customization, and the free price. The complaints were more practical than dramatic: payment setup can take work, navigation can feel crowded, reporting can become awkward, and a few reviewers described login, delivery, or support friction. That supports a clear trial rule. Build one real invoice, connect the payment path, send a reminder, and check that the customer receives it before moving every open account into the system.</p>

<p>FreshBooks' independent signal was mixed and thinner in this pass. The [Capterra page](https://www.capterra.com/p/142390/FreshBooks/reviews/) was behind a bot check, and [G2's FreshBooks page](https://www.g2.com/products/freshbooks/reviews) did not expose readable review detail. A linked <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/Bookkeeping/comments/1nrtu90/what_do_we_think_of_fresh_books/">r/Bookkeeping discussion</a> is not a contractor sample or a statistical verdict, but it shows the trade-off worth testing: one commenter praised support and training, while others criticized reconciliation, recurring-expense categorization, reporting, and price increases. That makes FreshBooks a workflow trial, not an automatic upgrade from a free invoicing tool.</p>

<p>There was no equally useful contractor-specific Reddit discussion for Zoho Invoice in the source check. The <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/Invoice/comments/1uuzqvp/zoho_invoice_subscription_is_it_worth_paying_for/">Zoho Invoice Reddit thread</a> is linked for buyers who want to read the community context, but it is not enough to claim a contractor consensus. The Capterra patterns and the official limits carry more of the buying case.</p>

## The failure modes are different

<p>FreshBooks fails a contractor who has outgrown Lite before noticing it. Five billable clients is a small ceiling, and the next listed public rate is $43/mo. That is not a reason to reject the product. It is a reason to count the live client list and avoid budgeting from a tier the business cannot use. The other FreshBooks failure is category mismatch: if materials, labor, subcontractors, and progress billing drive the financial decisions, the invoice workflow is too shallow.</p>

<p>Zoho Invoice fails when a contractor treats its $0 tier as a permanent operating system. The 2-user cap can be hit by an owner, office helper, and technician. Three projects can disappear quickly during a remodel, seasonal maintenance run, or a cluster of service work. The 500-invoice limit is a hard operational number, not a marketing footnote. The business needs a written trigger: at a defined invoice count, project count, or user count, compare the next system before the current one stops fitting.</p>

<p>Both products also fail when the office process is undefined. If nobody owns estimates, customer records, payment follow-up, and month-end review, a faster invoice app only sends the same scattered information in a nicer email. Fix ownership first, then use the trial to prove that the handoff works between the field, office, and bookkeeper.</p>

## Run the same contractor test in each product

<p>Use one completed job that has a real customer, a deposit or payment request, a few hours of labor, an expense, and a follow-up message. In FreshBooks, test the time or project record, expense entry, invoice, online payment request, and reminder. Then ask whether Lite's five-client limit is still realistic after the current pipeline is counted.</p>

<p>In Zoho Invoice, create an estimate, turn it into an invoice, set up the payment option the business actually accepts, send a reminder, and view the customer portal. Count each real user and active project at the same time. If the business will pass any published limit soon, use the $0 tier as a short test rather than treating it as the final destination.</p>

<p>A contractor who only needs the billing layer can also compare the rest of the field in the <a href="/best-invoicing-software-contractors/">invoicing software roundup</a>. A contractor who needs quotes, schedule changes, technicians, and payment collection connected to the job should compare a field-service product before adopting either tool.</p>

## Final decision: choose FreshBooks, choose Zoho Invoice, or skip both

<p><strong>Buy or shortlist FreshBooks</strong> if the business bills often enough that a paid invoice workflow is justified, needs more room than Lite's client ceiling, and can budget from the actual client and user count. Start with the <a href="/go/freshbooks-official-site/" rel="nofollow sponsored" target="_blank">FreshBooks pricing and trial path</a>, then test a real invoice cycle before committing.</p>

<p><strong>Buy or shortlist Zoho Invoice</strong> if the business is genuinely small, the $0 price protects cash, and 2 users, 3 projects, and 500 invoices a year leave comfortable operating room. Start with <a href="/go/zoho-invoice-official-site/" rel="nofollow sponsored" target="_blank">Zoho Invoice</a>, but put the first migration trigger on the calendar now.</p>

<p><strong>Skip both</strong> if the next decision is job costing, construction billing, AIA forms, retainage, scheduling, dispatch, or field-team workflow. That buyer does not need a prettier invoice app. They need a product category built around the work that happens before the invoice.</p>
